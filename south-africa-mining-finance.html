Mining tax and finance advisory in South Africa

South Africa's mining fiscal regime is stable but unusually technical. Most of the money is lost to classification, ring-fencing and apportionment errors that repeat every period until somebody recalculates them.

Where South African mining groups lose money

Unlike Ghana, South Africa did not rewrite its mining fiscal rules this year. The exposure here is not change — it is complexity that has been compounding quietly.

1. Refined versus unrefined classification

The royalty under the Mineral and Petroleum Resources Royalty Act, 2008 is formula-driven off earnings before interest and tax over gross sales, and there are two different formulas. The rate is capped at 5% for refined mineral resources and 7% for unrefined, with a floor of 0.5% in both cases.

Classify a stream incorrectly and you apply the wrong formula to the whole of it, every period, until someone checks. Where a group has both refined and unrefined output, EBIT has to be determined separately for each group of minerals, and shared allowable expenses apportioned between them — which is where the second layer of error usually sits. We cover this in detail in refined or unrefined?

2. Capital expenditure ring-fenced to the wrong mine

Section 36(7F) of the Income Tax Act ring-fences capital expenditure to a particular mine: that expenditure is restricted to the taxable income of that mine and cannot be deducted against the taxable income of other distinct mines the company owns. The Supreme Court of Appeal set out the ordering approach in Armgold/Harmony Freegold Joint Venture (Pty) Ltd v CSARS [2012] ZASCA 152.

There is relief: for a mine that commenced operations after 14 March 1990, excess unredeemed capital expenditure may be deducted against the total taxable income from the taxpayer's other mines, limited to 25% of that income. Groups that treat their mines as one pool, or that fail to use the 25% relief where it is available, get the timing of their tax payments materially wrong in one direction or the other.

3. Gross sales that do not reflect market value

Gross sales must reflect the market value of the mineral. Connected-party charges deducted from gross sales — freight, insurance, handling — have to be on arm's length terms and properly evidenced. For groups with an offshore marketing entity this is the single most likely place for a royalty adjustment to arise.

4. Gold mining's own tax formula

Companies mining for gold are taxed under the gold mining tax formula rather than the flat corporate rate: y = 34 − 170/x, where x is taxable income from gold mining expressed as a percentage of total revenue from gold mining. Because the rate is a function of margin, anything that misstates taxable income or revenue misstates the rate itself — errors do not stay proportionate.

5. Carbon tax has entered its second phase

Phase 1 of the Carbon Tax Act ended on 31 December 2025. Phase 2 began on 1 January 2026 and the headline rate rose to R308 per tonne of CO₂e, from R236. The basic tax-free allowance reduces in 2026, with further carbon fuel levy adjustments from 1 April 2026. Mining operations above the activity thresholds may have to report and account for emissions from fuel combustion, industrial processes and fugitive emissions including methane.

For most mining finance teams this has moved from an environmental reporting item to a tax line with a number against it.

How we work in South Africa

We work with South African mining groups from Accra, usually remotely with periodic visits, and most often alongside an existing local tax adviser rather than instead of one. The work that tends to add most is the recalculation: an independent rebuild of the royalty position and the capex ring-fencing, by somebody with no stake in the treatment already adopted.

  • Transaction support — where ring-fencing and classification move the price
  • Internal audit and risk

Scale of the market

Mining's direct contribution to South African GDP was approximately R439.2 billion, around 5.8% of the national total, with mineral exports of R813.6 billion. It remains one of the most technically demanding mining tax environments anywhere, which is precisely why the recurring errors are worth finding.
