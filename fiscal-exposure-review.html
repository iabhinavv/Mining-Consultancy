The 21-Day Mining Fiscal Exposure Review

Ghana rewrote its mining fiscal rules in 2026. This review tells you exactly what that did to your numbers, what your group actually owes, and what to fix first — in writing, in twenty-one days.

Book a 30-minute call What it covers

Who this is for

A mining group with operations in Ghana or South Africa, somewhere between two and fifteen legal entities, and a finance team that is already fully occupied closing the books. Typically gold, with revenue between $100M and $500M — though the review works at both ends of that range.

It is not for exploration companies with no production and no royalty obligation. If that's you, say so on the call and we will point you somewhere more useful.

Why now, specifically

This is not a manufactured deadline. Four things changed in Ghana this year, and the dates are fixed:

What changed In force
Flat 5% gold royalty replaced by a sliding scale reaching 12% (L.I. 2517) 10 Mar 2026
Growth and Sustainability Levy on gold mining cut from 3% to 1% 31 Mar 2026
E-invoicing mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses 1 Jul 2026
GRA's new tax administration platform (ITAS) national rollout Sep 2026
Minerals Commission audit: 10 years of production logs, 3 years of financial records Nov 2025 – Jun 2026

Your first full financial year under all of this closes in roughly two months. Every month that passes is another month of returns filed on assumptions that may no longer hold — and a longer remediation schedule when someone eventually checks. The full detail and sources are on the 2026 fiscal change register.

What the review covers

Four areas, each producing a written finding ranked by what it is worth.

  • 1. Royalty recalculationGhana & South Africa

    Every royalty return since the regime change, recalculated against the scale actually in force. In South Africa, the refined versus unrefined classification tested against the formula applied — a common and expensive error.

  • 2. Tax and filing exposureEvery entity

    Entity by entity, jurisdiction by jurisdiction: what was due, what was filed, what is open. Includes the GSL change, withholding, VAT and e-invoicing readiness.

  • 3. Consolidation integrityGroup level

    Where the group numbers stop agreeing with the entity numbers, which intercompany balances don't reconcile, and what an auditor would question first.

  • 4. Cash and price sensitivity13 weeks

    A rolling 13-week cash forecast, stress-tested at different commodity prices — including what the price move does to your royalty rate, now that the two are linked.

What you get at the end

  • A written findings report, ranked by financial exposure, not by page count
  • A remediation plan with owners, dates and an estimate of what each item is worth
  • A compliance calendar for every entity and jurisdiction
  • The 13-week cash model, as a working file you keep
  • A working session with your finance team, and a board-ready summary if you want one

What we need from you

This is the part most people get wrong about reviews like this, so it is worth being precise. We work from documents your finance team already produces:

  • Trial balance by entity, current and prior year
  • Royalty returns and supporting production and sales data
  • The group reporting pack and last signed audit file
  • Bank statements for the main operating accounts
  • A procurement spend extract, if the procurement module is in scope

No system access. No new reports built for our benefit. No workshops with your team blocked out for a week. If getting us these documents takes your financial controller more than half a day, we have scoped it wrong.

What we commit to

Scope and fee are agreed in writing before any work starts, so the number cannot move once we are inside.

Findings are delivered on the date we agree — or you don't pay the final instalment.

How it runs

  1. Day 0 — Call. Thirty minutes. Entities, jurisdictions, what is worrying you.
  2. Day 1–3 — Scope. A written scope, deliverables, timeline and fee. Nothing starts until you sign it. A confidentiality agreement if you want one.
  3. Day 4–7 — Documents. You send the list above. We come back with questions once, in a single batch.
  4. Day 8–18 — Review. We work. You don't hear from us except for a short mid-point note.
  5. Day 19–21 — Findings. Written report, then a working session.

What this costs

The fee depends on the number of entities and jurisdictions, and we quote it after the first call rather than guessing in public. What we can tell you now is how it is structured: a fixed amount agreed in advance, billed in two milestones, invoiced in USD.

For context on the other side of the equation: a single misapplied royalty band, or one misclassified mineral stream in South Africa, is routinely worth more than the whole review.

Common objections, answered

"Our auditors would have caught this."

A statutory audit gives an opinion on whether the financial statements are fairly stated. It is not designed to recalculate every royalty return against a regime that changed mid-year, and it is not looking for recoverable cost. Different question, different work.

"We have an in-house tax manager."

Good — the review will be faster and cheaper. The value is a second set of eyes with no stake in the decisions already made, and a view across every entity at once rather than one at a time.

"We don't have time for this right now."

That is the reason the scope is built the way it is. Half a day of your controller's time, no system access, no workshops. The alternative is finding out during the next audit, when the remediation window is someone else's to set.

"What if you find nothing?"

Then you get a written, evidenced statement that your royalty, tax and consolidation positions are sound — which is itself worth having in front of a board or a lender, and which we have never yet had to write.
