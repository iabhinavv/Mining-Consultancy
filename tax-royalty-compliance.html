Mining royalty and tax compliance

Work out what your group actually owes under Ghana's sliding-scale royalty and South Africa's formula-driven one — and close the filing gaps before somebody else finds them.

Book a 30-minute call The 21-Day Review

The problem

Royalty used to be the simplest line in a Ghanaian gold miner's tax position: five per cent, flat, every quarter. Since 10 March 2026 it moves with the gold price, from 5% up to 12% above $4,500 an ounce, under the Minerals and Mining (Royalties) Regulations, 2025 (L.I. 2517). Lithium moved to its own 5–12% scale at the same time.

That single change breaks more than the royalty return. It changes your accruals, your budget, your covenant headroom and your cash forecast — and it links your effective tax rate to the same commodity price that drives your revenue. Meanwhile the Growth and Sustainability Levy on gold mining dropped from 3% to 1% on 31 March 2026, which moves the number the other way, and e-invoicing became mandatory on 1 July 2026 with VAT registration suspension as the penalty for not onboarding.

In South Africa the problem is different and older. The royalty under the Mineral and Petroleum Resources Royalty Act is formula-driven off EBIT and gross sales, capped at 5% for refined and 7% for unrefined mineral resources. Get the refined versus unrefined classification wrong and you apply the wrong formula to the whole stream, every period, until someone checks.

What we do

We recalculate rather than review. Every royalty return since the regime change, rebuilt from your production and sales data against the scale actually in force in each period, and compared to what you filed. Then the same treatment for the rest of the position: corporate tax, withholding, VAT, the levy, and e-invoicing readiness, entity by entity.

Where there is a gap we quantify it before we recommend anything, because the size of the number decides whether it is worth fixing now, fixing at year end, or disclosing.

What you receive

  • Exposure map — every entity, every jurisdiction, every tax head, with an amount against each open item
  • Royalty recalculation file — the working model, which you keep and can re-run each period as the price moves
  • Compliance calendar — what is due, when, and who in your team owns it
  • Remediation plan — ranked by financial exposure, with an estimate of what each item is worth and what it costs to fix

How it is billed

Usually a fixed-fee project to establish the position, then a quarterly retainer to keep it current as the price moves through the royalty bands and the GRA's practice develops. Scope and fee agreed in writing before anything starts.

Who this is for

Producing mining companies with a royalty obligation in Ghana or South Africa, particularly groups filing in both. If you have a single entity, a single jurisdiction and an in-house tax manager who is on top of it, you probably don't need us — and we will say so on the call.

Questions

Is this legal advice?

No. This is financial and tax advisory work. Where a position needs legal sign-off — a dispute, an objection, a stabilisation agreement — we work alongside local counsel in the relevant country.

Do you deal with the GRA on our behalf?

We prepare the position, the schedules and the supporting argument, and we will sit with your team in a meeting. Formal representation is a matter for your tax agent or counsel.

What if we are already under audit?

Then say so on the first call, because it changes the sequencing and sometimes the advice. The Minerals Commission ran a physical and financial audit from November 2025 to June 2026 requiring ten years of production logs and three years of financial records; several groups are still working through what that surfaced.
