Working capital and cash flow forecasting

Know your cash position thirteen weeks out, and what happens to it when the price moves — including what the price now does to your royalty rate.

Book a 30-minute call The 21-Day Review

The problem

Mines are capital-intensive and cash-poor at exactly the wrong moments. Capex is committed, the payroll is fixed, the stripping schedule does not care what gold did last week — and revenue moves with a price you don't control.

Since March 2026 Ghanaian gold producers have a second exposure to the same price. The royalty rate itself now slides with it, from 5% below $2,000 an ounce up to 12% above $4,500. A rising price costs you a higher royalty rate; a falling price costs you revenue. Most cash models built before March assume a flat 5% and are quietly wrong in both directions.

The practical consequence is that a finance director who could previously answer "what happens if gold drops 15%?" with a revenue sensitivity now needs a model that moves the royalty band too.

What we do

We build a rolling 13-week direct cash forecast — receipts and payments, not an indirect derivation from the P&L — and we build the royalty band logic into it so the tax line responds to the price assumption rather than sitting as a fixed percentage.

Then we stress it. Base case, price up, price down, and whatever the specific worry is: a delayed VAT refund, a shipment held at the port, a covenant test date. The output is not a forecast you believe; it is a set of thresholds you watch.

What you receive

  • Rolling 13-week cash model — a working file your team updates weekly, not a one-off report
  • Price scenarios — with the royalty band moving correctly in each
  • Minimum cash line — the level below which decisions change, and the date you would hit it in each scenario
  • Working capital release plan — what can be freed from receivables, inventory and payables, ranked by how fast and how painful
  • Weekly update routine — a short process so the model stays alive after we leave

How it is billed

A fixed-fee project to build the model and the first set of scenarios, then an optional quarterly refresh as prices and the operating plan move. Many clients take the refresh inside a wider advisory retainer.

Who this is for

Producing miners with real working capital pressure, companies approaching a covenant test, and groups whose board has started asking for cash reporting the finance team cannot produce weekly. Also useful ahead of a refinancing, where the lender will ask for exactly this.

Questions

Can't our ERP do this?

Most can produce an indirect forecast from the ledger. Very few produce a weekly direct forecast with commodity price scenarios and a moving royalty band, which is the thing that actually answers the board's question.

Who maintains it afterwards?

Your team. That is the point of handing over a working file and a routine rather than a PDF. If you would rather we ran the weekly update, that is a retainer.

How long does it take?

The model itself is usually two to three weeks from receiving bank statements and the trial balance. It is one of the four workstreams in the 21-Day Review.
