Internal audit and risk framework

Give your board assurance over the risks that actually matter in mining — safety, environment, community, fraud and financial control — rather than the ones that are easy to test.

Book a 30-minute call The 21-Day Review

The problem

Mining carries risks that do not appear on a standard corporate audit plan. A fatality, a tailings incident, a community blockade, fuel theft at a remote site, a procurement officer with a relationship to a supplier — each is operationally obvious and financially enormous, and none of them is found by testing journal approvals.

Most mid-tier groups end up with one of two failure modes. Either there is no internal audit function at all and the board relies on the statutory auditor for comfort the statutory auditor was never engaged to give; or there is a function that produces a lot of activity against low-value risks because those are the ones with tidy test procedures.

What we do

We start with the risk register, because an audit plan that is not derived from a ranked risk register is just a list of habits. We build or rebuild it with the operating team, not only the finance team, and we rank by financial and operational exposure.

From that we design a risk-based audit plan, test the controls that matter, and report in a form an audit committee can act on: what we tested, what we found, what it is worth, who owns the fix and by when.

What you receive

  • Risk register — ranked by exposure, owned by named people, reviewed on a cycle
  • Risk-based internal audit plan — annual, with a clear rationale for what is in and what is out
  • Controls testing — fieldwork and findings, with evidence
  • Board and audit committee reporting — short, ranked, and written to be read
  • Follow-up tracker — so last quarter's findings don't reappear next year

How it is billed

Monthly or quarterly retainer, because assurance is a cycle rather than an event. Some groups take a one-off engagement to build the register and the plan, then run it in-house and bring us back for the annual refresh.

Who this is for

Groups with a board or audit committee asking for assurance, companies with a lender or investor covenant that requires an internal audit function, and operations where the procurement and fuel exposures are large enough to justify dedicated testing.

Questions

Is this the same as a statutory audit?

No, and the distinction matters. A statutory audit gives an opinion on the financial statements for external users and must be performed by a licensed audit firm. Internal audit is management and board assurance over controls and risk. Amrev does the second, not the first.

Do you replace our internal audit team?

More often we design the framework and your team runs it, or we co-source specific reviews that need skills you don't keep in-house. Fully outsourced is possible for smaller groups.

Can you test safety and environmental controls?

We can test whether the control framework exists, is operating, and is reported properly. Technical inspection — geotechnical, tailings, occupational hygiene — needs a specialist, and we will say so rather than pretend otherwise.
