Procurement and supply chain cost review

Find the savings in what your sites actually buy, and build alternative supply channels so a single vendor cannot set your cost base.

Book a 30-minute call The 21-Day Review

The problem

Fuel, reagents, grinding media, tyres, spares, drilling contractors, haulage, camp services. On most mid-tier operations these are bought site by site, by people who are judged on keeping the plant running rather than on price, from a supplier list that has not been competitively tested in years.

The result is predictable: nobody owns the total. Group finance sees a cost line, site management sees a relationship, and the number in between — what the group is actually paying per unit, across sites, compared to what it could pay — is not calculated by anyone.

This is also the cost base that matters most when the gold price falls. Revenue you don't control; procurement you do.

What we do

We start with spend analysis, because you cannot negotiate what you cannot see: every purchase order and invoice line for a defined period, cleaned, categorised and aggregated across sites and entities. That alone usually surprises people — the same item bought at three prices, or a "sole supplier" who is nothing of the sort.

From there we build a savings pipeline: consolidation of volumes across sites, retendering where the market is genuinely competitive, specification changes where the premium buys nothing, payment term improvements, and alternative supply channels where the current route is the expensive one.

Each item gets an owner, a date and a number, so the pipeline is a plan rather than a report.

What you receive

  • Spend analysis — cleaned and categorised across sites, entities and suppliers
  • Savings pipeline — each opportunity with an estimated value, an owner and a date
  • Supplier and contract recommendations — what to retender, renegotiate, or replace
  • Agreed baseline — the documented reference point that the success fee is measured against
  • Tracking sheet — so delivered savings are verified rather than claimed

How it is billed

A fixed fee for the analysis, plus a share of savings verified against the baseline we agree with you before work starts. You only pay the success element on savings that actually show up in your ledger, measured against a number you signed off.

We will not quote a percentage saving before seeing your spend data. Anybody who does is guessing.

Who this is for

Operations with more than one site, or a single large operation with meaningful consumable and contractor spend. The work needs reasonably complete purchase data; if your spend lives in paper requisitions, the first engagement is getting it into a form that can be analysed.

Questions

Will this damage our supplier relationships?

Not if it is done properly. Most savings come from volume consolidation, specification and payment terms rather than squeezing a price. Suppliers generally prefer a group contract to three uncoordinated site accounts.

How is a saving "verified"?

Against the documented baseline, on a like-for-like unit basis, visible in your own ledger. Price moves caused by the market rather than by the work are excluded in both directions.

Do you take commission from suppliers?

No. We are paid by you and only by you. A procurement adviser taking money from both sides cannot give honest advice to either.
