Group consolidation and audit readiness

Close faster across subsidiaries, and hand your statutory auditor a file that does not come back with a list of questions.

Book a 30-minute call The 21-Day Review

The problem

A mid-tier mining group is rarely one company. It is a holding entity, an operating company per mine, a procurement or marketing entity, sometimes a financing vehicle offshore — across two or more countries, in two or more currencies, often on two or more charts of accounts that grew separately.

The symptoms are always the same. Month-end close takes three or four weeks instead of one. The group numbers and the entity numbers disagree and nobody can say exactly where. Intercompany balances don't reconcile and get plugged. The audit arrives, asks for a schedule nobody has built, and the finance team spends six weeks answering questions instead of running the business.

Ghana's Minerals Commission audit made this concrete: three years of financial records, requested at once. Groups with a clean consolidation produced them. Groups without one discovered exactly how much of their reporting lived in one person's spreadsheet.

What we do

We start by reconciling the group to the entities and finding every break, rather than designing a new process on top of an unexamined one. Once the breaks are listed and explained, we build the consolidation properly: a mapped chart of accounts, a defined intercompany matrix, documented FX translation, and a reporting pack that produces the same answer every month without heroics.

Then we make it audit-ready, which mostly means building the schedules your auditor will ask for before they ask, and documenting the judgements so the answer does not depend on who is in the room.

What you receive

  • Consolidation process — documented, with a mapped chart of accounts and a defined close calendar
  • Intercompany reconciliation matrix — every balance, both sides, with the differences explained rather than plugged
  • Group reporting pack — the monthly output your board actually reads
  • Audit-ready schedules — the supporting files your statutory auditor will request, built in advance
  • Close checklist — owners and dates, so the process survives someone leaving

How it is billed

Normally an annual engagement, because the work follows your reporting cycle: establish the position, rebuild the process, then support the first two or three closes until it runs without us. Fixed fee, agreed in writing.

Who this is for

Groups with three or more legal entities, or any group reporting across more than one country. If you have a single entity and a clean trial balance, this is not your problem and we will tell you so.

Questions

Do you sign the audit opinion?

No. Amrev is not a licensed audit firm and does not sign statutory audit opinions. We prepare the group for audit and work alongside your registered auditor — who remains independent of us, as they should.

Do you need access to our ERP?

No. We work from the trial balances, the group pack and the last audit file. If you want us to design inside your system later, that is a separate conversation.

Which reporting framework?

IFRS in both Ghana and South Africa, which is where we work. If your group reports to a parent on a different framework, tell us on the call.
